Many of you may remember when Gina volunteered with Young Audiences, an organization that  made the world of music more accessable to high schoolers. I was impressed with her work there, both as a good cause and because she got up very early in the morning to do it.
Once, a student introduced her to another student as "the lady who throws parties for a living." I remember her saying "That's me! The lady who throws parties for a living."

As the Lady Who Throws Parties for a Living, one of Gina's finest inventions and most useful professional tools is the Gina No.

The Gina No, as many of us call it, starts out as a yes. There are no "no's" in the Ginaverse. There are only differing amounts of possibility in any given moment. When the frontmen and divas of the cutting-edge acts Gina has brought to Holocene for more than a decade demand $800 tables or  when booking agents expect her to have read emails they never sent, the Gina No meets them where they are. I'm happy to tell you what I think about your previous email if you could maybe hit send on it. 

To be a friend of Gina is to benefit from the talents and work of the Lady Who Throws Parties for a Living. And the Gina No really shines there, because it's just the tail end of an infinite series of "yes's". It's how you learn that there's actually plenty of time to stop for a cupcake. It's how new traditions grow out of crazy ideas, like what if Holocene dressed up as the Bronze, the nighclub from Buffy the Vampire Slayer, every Halloween? Many of us have thrown out a crazy idea in the moment that Gina meets with a "Could do" or a "Fun!" with a gentle followup that guides it back on the rails, such as "but that might be hard since that bar closed last year" or, a current fav, "is it fun for everyone?" The Gina No is a embrace of possibility, and humanity, and fallability. It's the ability to say, when confronted with the fact that Third Eye Blind's "Semi Charmed Life" is an ode to crystal meth from a verifiably toxic man, "I'm sure it was lots of fun for him."  It is a way to recognize that The Heart Wants What the Heart Wants, That We are But Flesh and Bone. And, sometimes, Things Happen.

The Gina No is not bland acquiescence. The Gina No is really just one facet of a Neitzschean affirmation that Gina carries through life like a frilly parasol. It routes the determination required to keep the party going away from the grind and back to the fun.  It protects the deeply held care that Gina has for those around her. So, of course, there are things Gina does say No to. Parallel parking. Bumper cars. Soccer balls being kicked near or towards her head. Looooots of stuff Josh and Merlin do. And, most importantly, she said no to losing faith in Portland's ability to deliver a good man. Talk about determination!

I met Josh the night before my wife and I moved across the country during the pandemic. Because all the Renaissance Faires had been canceled, Andrea decided to throw one in her back yard, so that was the setting for our last hang in Portland, which Gina was trying to turn into our first hang with Josh. I don't remember what Gina had been saying about this guy she had only gone on one or two dates with, but it wasn't... the usual. 

Gina kept saying you gotta meet this guy, I have a really good feeling about him. Maybe I can get him to come out. It was getting late, this guy was gonna have to roll up to a big in-joke of a party and meet a dozen of Gina's friends (most of which were in costume) all at once. After like the second date. I remember thinking no way this guy's gonna show up. If he does, that's some real Gina energy.

Of course he showed up. In a Hawaiian shirt. He sat by Gina and laughed at all the nonsense going on and had some of his own to share, appropriately nervous but still clearly himself. And Gina looked *delighted.* As I heard more and more about this guy Josh in the months that followed, I only had that moment of Josh to picture, and it seemed brimming with possiblity.

I didn't get a chance to really hang with Josh until the next summer, when we visited and went camping. I still had my instincts to bring extra of everything when camping with Gina, as she was notorious for only packing pillows, fancy hats, and champagne. But here was this Josh guy! And he had *everything.* He even had dry ice for the coolers, which, we all learned, had the ability to carbonate basically any food. Without Josh I would never have tasted a carbonated carrot.

I think that was the moment I really saw it. Gina had long maintained, rightly, that bringing the pillows, fancy hats, and champagne filled an important niche in the ecology of partying, and that it complemented the practical stuff, which was the responsibility of Other People. Let's be clear -- as the Lady Who Throws Parties, Gina is not impractical, but she has a highly specific set of fun-oriented practical skills. These build community, they keep us laughing, and maintain openness to possibility. One cannot just comlement them with anodyne practicaility. It requires a rare combination of madness and responsibility, an unhinged readiness to meet the unexpected. It requires a Josh. 

Josh brought more than headlamps and extra flannels to his adventures with Gina. He brought his own trademark affirmation. I don't know if it's a word. It's a kind of ripply laugh that rolls into a "sure" or a "why not." It betrays amusement at the unexpected and a readiness to meet it, whether it's a backyard rennaisance faire or a little monster named Merlin. Since the moment we met, I have been impressed and thankful for his ability to match the irreplacable energy of my dear, dear friend Gina as they build a family and make their way through this crazy thing we call life together.

So, as is tradition for wedding speeches, I will end with a quote from Friedrich Neitszche:

```Suppose that we said yes to a single moment, then we have not only said yes to ourselves, but to the whole of existence. For nothing stands alone, either in ourselves or in things; and if our soul did but once vibrate and resound with a chord of happiness, then all of eternity was necessary to bring forth this one occurrence—and in this single moment when we said yes, all of eternity was embraced, redeemed, justified and affirmed.```
